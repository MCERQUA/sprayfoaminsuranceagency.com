26:T1587,<h1>Monsoon-Proofing Your Arizona Home: A Pre-Season Checklist That Protects Your Coverage</h1>
<p>Arizona's monsoon officially begins June 15, and the storms that follow are the biggest threat your home faces all year: microburst winds, flash flooding, driving dust, and lightning. The good news is that a weekend of preparation each spring dramatically reduces your risk of a costly loss — and, just as importantly, keeps your home in the good standing that carriers reward.</p>
<p>Insurance covers sudden accidental damage. It does <em>not</em> cover damage that results from deferred maintenance. A roof that fails because it was neglected, or water that intrudes through a gap you could have sealed, can lead to a denied claim. This checklist protects you on both fronts: fewer losses, and stronger claims when a loss does happen.</p>
<h2>Roof and Attic</h2>
<p>Your roof is your first line of defense and your most claim-prone component.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Inspect the roof</strong> (or have a pro do it) for cracked, loose, or missing tiles and shingles. Monsoon winds exploit every weak point.</li>
<li><strong>Check and re-seal flashing</strong> around vents, skylights, and chimneys — a common leak source.</li>
<li><strong>Clear roof valleys and scuppers</strong> of debris so water flows freely.</li>
<li><strong>Photograph your roof now, in good condition.</strong> A dated "before" record is powerful evidence if a storm causes damage later.</li>
<li><strong>Trim overhanging branches</strong> that could break off and puncture the roof.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Drainage and Flash-Flood Defense</h2>
<p>Flooding is excluded from your homeowners policy, so prevention here protects money insurance won't return.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Clear gutters and downspouts</strong> and make sure downspouts discharge <em>away</em> from the foundation.</li>
<li><strong>Regrade low spots</strong> where water pools against the house.</li>
<li><strong>Clear nearby washes and drainage easements</strong> of debris that could redirect runoff toward your home.</li>
<li><strong>Consider sandbags or flood barriers</strong> if you're in a low-lying area or near a wash — and know where to get them before a storm, not during one.</li>
<li><strong>If you don't have flood insurance, get a quote now.</strong> NFIP policies carry a 30-day waiting period, so waiting until a storm is in the forecast is too late.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Windows, Doors, and Exterior</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Re-caulk and weatherstrip</strong> windows and exterior doors to block wind-driven rain and dust.</li>
<li><strong>Secure or store loose yard items</strong> — patio furniture, umbrellas, potted plants, trampolines, and grills all become projectiles in a microburst.</li>
<li><strong>Inspect and reinforce block walls and fences,</strong> which monsoon winds routinely topple.</li>
<li><strong>Check that your garage door</strong> is rated and functioning; a failed garage door can lead to catastrophic wind entry.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Trees and Landscaping</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Trim and thin large trees,</strong> especially those near the house, power lines, or your roof.</li>
<li><strong>Check that mature trees are healthy</strong> and well-rooted; stressed desert trees fall easily in saturated soil.</li>
<li><strong>Remove dead limbs and cactus arms</strong> that could become airborne.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Electrical, HVAC, and Systems</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Install whole-home surge protection</strong> to guard against lightning-driven power surges.</li>
<li><strong>Service your AC condenser</strong> and consider a hail guard — condensers are a frequent, expensive hail casualty.</li>
<li><strong>Know how to shut off</strong> your water, gas, and electricity in an emergency.</li>
<li><strong>Back up important electronics</strong> to surge-protected circuits or unplug them when severe storms approach.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Documentation and Insurance Review</h2>
<p>This is the step homeowners skip — and regret.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Create or update a home inventory:</strong> photos or video of every room, plus receipts for high-value items. Store it in the cloud so it survives a loss.</li>
<li><strong>Review your policy before the season.</strong> Confirm your roof settlement basis (replacement cost vs. actual cash value), your dwelling limit versus true rebuild cost, your other-structures limit for pools and casitas, and your loss-of-use coverage.</li>
<li><strong>Confirm your flood coverage</strong> is in force with the waiting period already satisfied.</li>
<li><strong>Save your carrier's claims number</strong> in your phone now, so it's there when you need it.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Build an Emergency Kit</h2>
<p>Monsoon storms cause power outages and can strand you at home. Keep flashlights, batteries, water, non-perishable food, a battery or crank radio, phone chargers, and a basic first-aid kit accessible.</p>
<h2>A Little Prevention, a Lot of Protection</h2>
<p>None of these steps is expensive or difficult, but together they can be the difference between a storm you barely notice and a five-figure claim. And the homeowners who prepare aren't just safer — they're the ones carriers most want to keep insuring.</p>
