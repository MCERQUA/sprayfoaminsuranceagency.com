# Why Arizona Homeowners Are Getting Non-Renewed — and What to Do About It

Few pieces of mail cause as much anxiety as a non-renewal notice from your homeowners insurer. If you've received one — or you're worried you might — take a breath. A non-renewal is not a catastrophe, and it's not a reflection of anything you did wrong. It's a symptom of a hardening Arizona insurance market, and there is a clear path back to coverage.

## Non-Renewal vs. Cancellation — They're Not the Same

First, an important distinction:

- **Cancellation** happens *mid-term* and is rare. In Arizona it's generally limited to specific reasons like non-payment or material misrepresentation on your application.
- **Non-renewal** happens *at the end of your policy term*, when the carrier simply chooses not to offer you a new policy. Your current coverage stays in force until the expiration date on the notice.

Arizona law requires insurers to give you advance written notice of non-renewal — typically well before your policy expires — which gives you time to line up replacement coverage. That window is your friend. Use it.

## Why It's Happening Across Arizona

The wave of non-renewals isn't personal. Several forces are squeezing the market at once:

**Wildfire losses.** Catastrophic wildfire seasons across the West have driven massive insurer losses. Carriers have responded by tightening or exiting wildland-urban interface (WUI) areas — which in Arizona means Flagstaff, Prescott, Sedona, Payson, and foothill neighborhoods around the state.

**Roof claims and sun damage.** Arizona's sun is merciless on roofs. Carriers have paid out heavily on roof claims and have grown wary of older roofs, in some cases non-renewing homes whose roofs have exceeded a certain age.

**Reinsurance costs.** The global cost carriers pay to insure *themselves* has risen sharply, and those costs flow downhill to homeowners in the form of higher premiums and stricter underwriting.

**Claims frequency.** Homes with multiple recent claims — even weather claims outside your control — are more likely to be non-renewed.

**Carrier appetite shifts.** Sometimes a carrier simply decides to stop writing a certain type of home, a certain area, or a certain risk class. Thousands of policyholders can be non-renewed at once, regardless of their individual record.

## What to Do the Moment You Get a Notice

Act early and methodically:

1. **Read the notice carefully.** Note the exact expiration date and any stated reason. That date is your deadline to secure replacement coverage without a lapse.
2. **Don't panic-buy the first replacement you find.** A non-renewal is the ideal moment to shop the whole market, not to grab whatever's fastest.
3. **Call an independent agent immediately.** This is where an independent agency earns its keep. We're not tied to one carrier — we can shop 12+ A-rated markets, including specialty and surplus-lines carriers that write homes standard insurers decline.
4. **Address the underlying issue if you can.** If the non-renewal cited an aging roof, getting a new roof (or an inspection showing remaining life) can dramatically expand your options. If it cited wildfire risk, defensible-space improvements and a current wildfire-mitigation report can help.
5. **Avoid a coverage lapse at all costs.** Bind your new policy to take effect on or before your current policy's expiration. A lapse can raise your future rates, violate your mortgage terms, and leave you exposed.

## Why a Lapse Is So Dangerous

If your mortgage lender discovers you're uninsured, they can purchase **force-placed insurance** on your behalf — coverage that is far more expensive than a normal policy and protects the lender, not you or your belongings. Beyond that, any loss during a lapse is entirely out of your pocket, and future carriers view a lapse as a red flag that raises your rate. The goal is always a seamless transition with no gap.

## What If Standard Carriers Won't Write My Home?

Even in the hardest cases, there are almost always options:

- **Surplus-lines carriers** specialize in risks that standard "admitted" carriers decline — older homes, WUI properties, and homes with claims history. Premiums are higher, but coverage is real.
- **The Arizona FAIR Plan** exists as a market of last resort for homes that can't get coverage elsewhere. It's more limited and more expensive than standard coverage, but it prevents a lapse and can be paired with a separate liability policy.
- **Mitigation-based placement.** For wildfire non-renewals in particular, documented defensible space, ember-resistant vents, and a hardened roof can move a home from "uninsurable" to "insurable" with the right carrier.

## Prevent the Next Non-Renewal

Once you're re-covered, a few habits reduce the odds of going through this again:

- **Keep your roof in good shape** and documented — it's the number-one underwriting flashpoint in Arizona.
- **Think twice before filing small claims.** Frequency matters more than size; a string of minor claims can cost you your policy.
- **Maintain defensible space** if you're anywhere near wildland.
- **Re-shop annually** with an independent agent so you're never dependent on a single carrier's appetite.
