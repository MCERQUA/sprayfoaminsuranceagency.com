How Much Is Homeowners Insurance in Arizona? 2026 Cost Breakdown by City

By Josh Cotner

How Much Is Homeowners Insurance in Arizona? 2026 Cost Breakdown by City

"Why is my Arizona home insurance so expensive?" is one of the most-searched insurance questions in the state — and for good reason. Premiums have climbed sharply over the last few years as carriers absorb wildfire losses, monsoon claims, roof-replacement costs, and construction-cost inflation.

So what should you actually expect to pay? The honest answer is: it depends on a handful of specific factors. But we can give you real ranges and show you exactly what moves the number.

The Statewide Range

For a typical single-family Arizona home, homeowners insurance in 2026 generally runs $1,200 to $2,500 per year, with plenty of homes falling above and below that band. Lower-risk, newer homes in the Phoenix and Tucson metros often come in under $1,200. Older homes, high-value custom properties, and anything in a wildfire-interface area can run well past $2,500.

Those numbers are averages, not quotes. The only way to know your real cost is to compare carriers on your specific home — which is exactly what an independent agent does.

Cost by Arizona City

Location is one of the biggest single drivers of your premium. Here's roughly how Arizona's major markets compare:

  • Phoenix metro (Phoenix, Mesa, Tempe, Glendale, Peoria): Among the more affordable markets in the state thanks to newer housing stock, low wildfire exposure in the urban core, and strong carrier competition. Many homes land in the $1,000–$1,800 range.
  • Scottsdale & Paradise Valley: Higher home values push premiums up, and desert-hillside properties near the McDowell Mountains carry wildfire-interface exposure. High-value homes here often need specialty carriers.
  • Tucson & Southern Arizona: Broadly competitive, though older housing stock and foothills wildfire risk can raise rates on specific properties.
  • Chandler, Gilbert & the East Valley: Newer subdivisions with favorable risk profiles — often some of the lowest premiums in the state, though renewal creep can quietly erode that advantage.
  • Flagstaff, Prescott & Northern Arizona: The most expensive market in the state. Wildland-urban interface (WUI) exposure, older wood-frame homes, and carriers exiting the region all drive premiums up, and some homes require surplus-lines placement.

What Actually Drives Your Premium

Beyond location, carriers weigh a specific set of factors when they price your policy:

Dwelling rebuild cost. The single biggest factor. The more it costs to rebuild your home, the more coverage you need and the higher your premium. Note this is rebuild cost, not market value.

Roof age and material. Arizona's brutal sun degrades roofs, and roof claims are a leading loss for carriers. An older roof — or one insured at actual cash value rather than replacement cost — heavily influences both your rate and your settlement.

Wildfire risk. Homes in or near WUI zones face higher premiums, stricter underwriting, and sometimes limited carrier availability.

Claims history. Multiple claims in a short window — yours or the home's prior owners' — raise your rate and can trigger non-renewal.

Construction type and age. Block and stucco homes, newer builds, and updated electrical/plumbing/roofing generally price better than older wood-frame construction.

Liability exposure. Pools, trampolines, and certain dog breeds increase liability risk and can nudge premiums up.

Deductible. A higher deductible lowers your premium — but make sure you can comfortably cover it after a loss.

How to Lower Your Arizona Home Insurance Premium

You have more control than you might think:

  • Shop multiple carriers. This is the highest-leverage move. Rates for the same home can vary by hundreds of dollars between carriers. We compare 12+ A-rated carriers on every quote.
  • Bundle home and auto. Typically saves 5–15% and simplifies renewals.
  • Raise your deductible — if you can absorb it.
  • Upgrade your roof. Impact-resistant roofing can earn meaningful discounts and improve your settlement terms.
  • Add a monitored alarm and water-leak sensors. Security and water-mitigation devices often earn 5–15% discounts.
  • Re-shop at renewal. Premiums creep upward every year. An annual re-market prevents you from silently overpaying.

Why "Cheap" Isn't the Goal — "Right" Is

It's tempting to chase the lowest number, but the cheapest Arizona policy is often the one that leaves you exposed: a low dwelling limit, an actual-cash-value roof, no flood coverage, and thin liability limits. When a monsoon microburst or wildfire hits, those savings evaporate.

The goal is the right coverage at the best available price — a policy that fully rebuilds your home, covers Arizona's real perils, and comes from a financially strong carrier that pays claims fairly.
