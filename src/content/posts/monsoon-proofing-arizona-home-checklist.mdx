# Monsoon-Proofing Your Arizona Home: A Pre-Season Checklist That Protects Your Coverage

Arizona's monsoon officially begins June 15, and the storms that follow are the biggest threat your home faces all year: microburst winds, flash flooding, driving dust, and lightning. The good news is that a weekend of preparation each spring dramatically reduces your risk of a costly loss — and, just as importantly, keeps your home in the good standing that carriers reward.

Insurance covers sudden accidental damage. It does *not* cover damage that results from deferred maintenance. A roof that fails because it was neglected, or water that intrudes through a gap you could have sealed, can lead to a denied claim. This checklist protects you on both fronts: fewer losses, and stronger claims when a loss does happen.

## Roof and Attic

Your roof is your first line of defense and your most claim-prone component.

- **Inspect the roof** (or have a pro do it) for cracked, loose, or missing tiles and shingles. Monsoon winds exploit every weak point.
- **Check and re-seal flashing** around vents, skylights, and chimneys — a common leak source.
- **Clear roof valleys and scuppers** of debris so water flows freely.
- **Photograph your roof now, in good condition.** A dated "before" record is powerful evidence if a storm causes damage later.
- **Trim overhanging branches** that could break off and puncture the roof.

## Drainage and Flash-Flood Defense

Flooding is excluded from your homeowners policy, so prevention here protects money insurance won't return.

- **Clear gutters and downspouts** and make sure downspouts discharge *away* from the foundation.
- **Regrade low spots** where water pools against the house.
- **Clear nearby washes and drainage easements** of debris that could redirect runoff toward your home.
- **Consider sandbags or flood barriers** if you're in a low-lying area or near a wash — and know where to get them before a storm, not during one.
- **If you don't have flood insurance, get a quote now.** NFIP policies carry a 30-day waiting period, so waiting until a storm is in the forecast is too late.

## Windows, Doors, and Exterior

- **Re-caulk and weatherstrip** windows and exterior doors to block wind-driven rain and dust.
- **Secure or store loose yard items** — patio furniture, umbrellas, potted plants, trampolines, and grills all become projectiles in a microburst.
- **Inspect and reinforce block walls and fences,** which monsoon winds routinely topple.
- **Check that your garage door** is rated and functioning; a failed garage door can lead to catastrophic wind entry.

## Trees and Landscaping

- **Trim and thin large trees,** especially those near the house, power lines, or your roof.
- **Check that mature trees are healthy** and well-rooted; stressed desert trees fall easily in saturated soil.
- **Remove dead limbs and cactus arms** that could become airborne.

## Electrical, HVAC, and Systems

- **Install whole-home surge protection** to guard against lightning-driven power surges.
- **Service your AC condenser** and consider a hail guard — condensers are a frequent, expensive hail casualty.
- **Know how to shut off** your water, gas, and electricity in an emergency.
- **Back up important electronics** to surge-protected circuits or unplug them when severe storms approach.

## Documentation and Insurance Review

This is the step homeowners skip — and regret.

- **Create or update a home inventory:** photos or video of every room, plus receipts for high-value items. Store it in the cloud so it survives a loss.
- **Review your policy before the season.** Confirm your roof settlement basis (replacement cost vs. actual cash value), your dwelling limit versus true rebuild cost, your other-structures limit for pools and casitas, and your loss-of-use coverage.
- **Confirm your flood coverage** is in force with the waiting period already satisfied.
- **Save your carrier's claims number** in your phone now, so it's there when you need it.

## Build an Emergency Kit

Monsoon storms cause power outages and can strand you at home. Keep flashlights, batteries, water, non-perishable food, a battery or crank radio, phone chargers, and a basic first-aid kit accessible.

## A Little Prevention, a Lot of Protection

None of these steps is expensive or difficult, but together they can be the difference between a storm you barely notice and a five-figure claim. And the homeowners who prepare aren't just safer — they're the ones carriers most want to keep insuring.
